How it works

Three passes, not three resumes.

Keep the canonical record clean. The version you send is a draft on top of it.

  1. 01

    Write it once.

    Basics, contact details, experience, education, skills, and projects live in one structured workspace. Add field notes where a fact needs context.

  2. 02

    Aim it.

    Paste a job description and describe the tone, density, and visual direction. The same facts get reordered around what matters for this role.

  3. 03

    Iterate on the render.

    Ask for a tighter layout, a different emphasis, or a warmer tone. The preview updates in place, and the source record stays untouched.

Principles

Your experience is not a prompt.

The point is not to generate more words. It is to present the record you already have with more intention.

01

Facts stay fixed.

No invented employers, dates, metrics, or credentials. The renderer can hide, merge, reorder, and rewrite existing material, but it cannot make up a career.

02

One record, many angles.

Write the resume once, then give each target its own emphasis, density, and hierarchy. The underlying record does not fork just because the version does.

03

Presentation is a layer.

The layout, type, emphasis, and tone belong to the version. The structured data belongs to you. Change the target without rewriting your history.

Ready when you are

The resume is already yours. Give it a better draft.
